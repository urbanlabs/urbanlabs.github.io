Credibility comes from knowing what it really takes to build and maintain a tool, leverage and govern a data standard, use analytics in a public-facing project, and create a scientifically-backed evidence base. It's not about shiny objects or a hot new tool. It's about things that work in context. Elizabeth keeps at least one hands-on project in the mix so her judgment about tools, methods, and engineering practice stays grounded in real work rather than buzzwords.

### Software Development

Experience spanning Python, data engineering, transit data pipelines, model systems, product-minded systems thinking, and the practical realities of public-sector technology delivery.

### Data Standards

Leadership and participation in efforts such as GTFS guidance, TIDES, TODS, the Mobility Data Interoperability Principles, and specification-oriented tooling layered around GMNS and GTFS.

### Planning Under Uncertainty

Long range planning needs direction, imagination, and humility. Models should not be judged by whether they precisely predict the far future, but by whether they help people reason more clearly about tradeoffs, possibilities, and the future they actually want to build.

### Scientific Methods

A wide toolbox of statistics, econometrics, machine learning, network analysis, optimization, survey design, ETL, and social-science methods used in real policy and delivery contexts.

### Transit Technology

Technical credibility grounded in real implementations, advisory roles, panel service, and sustained engagement with how transit technology succeeds or fails in practice.
